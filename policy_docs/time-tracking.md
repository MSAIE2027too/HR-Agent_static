# Time Tracking Policy

## Who tracks time

Non-exempt employees log hours daily; exempt employees log PTO, leave, and holidays only. Contractors follow their agreements. Everyone's entries are due by Monday noon for the prior week.

## How to log

Log start, end, and unpaid breaks in the HR portal to the quarter hour. Tag hours to project codes where assigned; overhead defaults to the team code. Same-day corrections are free; later corrections need manager approval with a note.

## Overtime linkage

Logged hours feed the overtime calculation under the Compensation and Payroll Policy: beyond 40 hours at 1.5 times the rate with pre-approval. Unlogged overtime is still paid once discovered, then addressed separately. Managers review and lock timesheets by Tuesday noon.

## Remote and travel days

Remote days are logged as normal workdays with the location tag. Travel days count actual hours including transit over 2 hours; red-eyes count per the Travel Policy. Per-diem days reconcile against logged travel days monthly.

## Audits and retention

Timesheets are retained 3 years. Payroll audits a sample monthly; falsification is a terminable offense, while honest mistakes corrected promptly carry no penalty. Holiday closures auto-fill; no entry needed.

## Frequently asked questions

Do I log lunch? Unpaid breaks of 30+ minutes, yes, as break time. What about training days? Log as work with the training code. Who approves corrections? Your manager, with a one-line reason.

## Timesheet correction procedure

Same-day fixes need no approval. Later fixes need manager approval with a one-line reason, filed before Tuesday lock. Post-lock fixes need HR payroll approval and appear as adjustments in the next cycle. Three corrections in a quarter trigger a helpful workflow review, not discipline.

## Manager review duties

Managers verify completeness (all days present), plausibility (hours match known schedules), and coding (project tags where assigned) before Tuesday noon lock. They chase missing sheets Monday afternoon and approve corrections within 2 business days. Unlock requests after payroll close need HR approval.

## Worked examples

Example A: a non-exempt week of 43 logged hours with pre-approval. Three hours pay at 1.5 times the rate on the next stub, tagged to the project code.

Example B: a missed Monday-noon deadline. Manager reminder Monday afternoon, employee files Tuesday morning, manager approves the late entry, payroll includes it normally.

Example C: a remote Friday logged with the location tag and standard hours. Matches the Remote Work Policy record; no PTO drawn, no overtime triggered.

## Project coding standards

Use assigned project codes for client and capital work; team overhead takes the default code. Split days across codes by actual hours, not vibes. Code changes after Tuesday lock need manager plus finance acknowledgment because cost allocation already ran.

## Exempt time norms

Exempt staff log only exceptions: PTO blocks, leave spans, holidays taken, and travel days. Full-week presence needs no entry. Partial-day personal appointments under 2 hours need no entry either, with manager awareness as the only rule.

## Apprentices and interns

Hourly interns log like non-exempt staff with mentor countersign weekly. Overtime for interns is prohibited; schedules cap at 40 hours. Timesheet training is part of intern week 1 per the Onboarding Policy.

## Records integration

Approved timesheets feed payroll, overtime, project costing, and per-diem reconciliation in one pass. Disputes reference the locked sheet plus the correction trail. No parallel shadow timesheets are recognized anywhere.

## Definitions glossary

Timesheet is the weekly portal record due Monday noon. Tuesday lock is the manager verification deadline feeding payroll. Project code tags hours to client or capital work; overhead takes the team default. Correction trail is the visible history of post-entry changes with reasons. Quarter-hour rounding is the logging granularity for start, end, and breaks.

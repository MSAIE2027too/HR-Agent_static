# Performance Reviews Policy

## Cycle

Reviews run annually: goal-setting in Q1, a midpoint check-in, and ratings in Q4. Goals are written jointly by employee and manager, measurable, and stored in the HR portal. Mid-year scope changes trigger a goal refresh, not a silent carryover.

## Ratings and calibration

Ratings use a 4-point scale with written justification per rating. Managers calibrate within their org to keep standards consistent; HR audits for bias patterns yearly. Employees see their rating and justification before it finalizes and may attach a response.

## Goal process

Each employee holds 3 to 5 goals mixing delivery, quality, and growth. Goals name the evidence that will prove them. Missed goals get a root-cause note, not just a lower mark, so the next cycle starts informed.

## Improvement plans

Plans last 3 months with written milestones and biweekly check-ins. Support (training, mentoring, adjusted scope) is documented alongside expectations. Remote-work eligibility pauses during a plan. Successful closure restores full standing; the record notes completion, not the plan itself, after 1 year.

## Linkage to pay and promotion

Ratings feed the bonus and promotion decisions under the Compensation and Payroll Policy. Top ratings accelerate review for promotion; bottom ratings require a plan before any promotion consideration. Bonus targets are set from the same goals.

## New hires and transfers

Employees joining after mid-year get a light first cycle: goals plus narrative feedback, with no forced distribution. Internal transfers carry their goals with manager agreement on any reset.

## Frequently asked questions

Can I dispute a rating? Yes, written response first, then HR review within 3 weeks. Do peers give input? Up to 3 peer perspectives may be attached by mutual agreement. What happens to goals when teams reorganize? They transfer or reset with both managers' sign-off.

## Calibration in detail

Managers meet by org with anonymized rating distributions and justification samples. Outliers get challenged with evidence, not seniority. HR tracks year-over-year movement for bias patterns and publishes the aggregate (never names) to leadership. Calibration never invents a forced curve; the 4-point scale stands on its justifications.

## Goal-writing guide

Good goals name the outcome, the evidence, and the date: "cut support response median to under 4 hours by Q3, proven by the dashboard export." Each goal lists its dependencies up front so misses produce learning instead of blame. Growth goals (a course, a rotation, a talk) count equally with delivery goals.

## Worked examples

Example A: an Analyst meeting all goals with clean evidence earns the standard rating with bonus linkage attached. Example B: an Engineer missing a goal for a canceled project gets the goal reset with both managers' sign-off rather than a miss on record. Example C: a plan case with met milestones closes successfully and the completion note replaces the plan in the active record after 1 year.

## Midpoint check-in format

Thirty minutes, three questions: what is on track, what drifted and why, what support unlocks the rest. Notes go in the portal within 2 business days. No ratings at midpoint, only trajectory language: ahead, on track, or needs focus with named support.

## Evidence standards

Delivery claims cite shipped artifacts, dashboards, or tickets. Quality claims cite review records, incident absence, or customer signals. Growth claims cite completed courses, rotations, or talks given. Assertions without retrievable evidence are marked unproven, not failed, with one cycle to cure the record-keeping.

## Underperformance handling before plans

A needs-focus trajectory triggers support first: clarified scope, training, mentoring, or load adjustment, documented with dates. Formal plans start only when support plus one full cycle did not move the trajectory. Skipping straight to a plan needs HR approval with written cause.

## Records and portability

Reviews, plans, and calibration notes live in the HR record. Transferring employees carry goals and trajectory; completed plans older than 1 year do not transfer as warnings. Employees may export their own review history anytime.

## Promotion packet guide

A promotion packet contains the sustained next-level evidence across two cycles, the business need statement from the manager, peer perspectives by mutual agreement, and the calibration outcome. Packets file in Q1 or Q3 windows; off-window packets need VP approval with cause. Denied packets return with the specific gap (evidence depth, tenure, business need) and a dated revisit, typically 2 cycles out. There is no appeal beyond one HR review of process fairness; outcomes themselves are final for the cycle.

## Definitions glossary

Four-point scale is the rating ladder with written justification per mark. Calibration is the cross-manager consistency session with anonymized distributions. Improvement plan is the 3-month milestone track with biweekly check-ins. Trajectory language (ahead, on track, needs focus) is the rating-free midpoint vocabulary. Growth goal is the learning objective counting equally with delivery goals.
